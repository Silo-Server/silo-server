# Writing patterns

Use this as a diagnostic list, not as a mechanical ban list. Preserve a construction when it is the clearest accurate choice.

## Cut or replace

- Empty praise, promotional adjectives, and grand claims.
- Stock openings and closers such as "Certainly", "Great question", "I hope this helps", and "Let me know if".
- Vague attribution such as "experts say" when no source is named.
- Generic framing that previews the answer instead of giving it.
- Forced groups of three, false ranges, and symmetrical "not only X but Y" constructions.
- Repeated bold labels that merely restate the sentence that follows.
- Abstract metaphors where a concrete engineering term exists.
- Dense sentences that make the reader backtrack.
- Adverbs that stand in for a measurement or stronger verb.
- Synonym cycling. Give one concept one name.
- Private vocabulary: names the writer coined while working that the reader has never seen. Use the established name or plain words.
- Restating what the reader already has, such as the diff, a linked issue, or code comments.
- Process narrative: how the writer got to the answer, when the reader needs only the answer and its evidence.

## Prefer

- The answer or outcome first.
- Specific actors and mechanisms.
- Measured change over adjectives such as "significant".
- Plain words such as "use", "help", and "if".
- Sentence-case headings and restrained formatting.
- A concise plain-English explanation before policy or implementation detail when both matter.
- Honest uncertainty where the evidence is incomplete.

## Final audit

Ask:

1. Could this paragraph appear unchanged in an unrelated project? If so, make it specific or cut it.
2. Would a reader who was not part of the work understand the first paragraph? If not, rewrite it in plain words.
3. Did style editing alter a fact, quote, citation, or confidence level? Restore it.
4. Does the text sound polished but anonymous? Add the author's actual judgment only when context supplies it.
5. Did punctuation or formatting become a visible mannerism? Simplify it.
